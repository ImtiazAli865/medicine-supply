# ADR-0007: Store Ledger and Account Summary (one append-only ledger, computed balance and allocation, exactly-once keys, one lock order, snapshot on the invoice)

> **Scope**: One decision cluster made in Module 007 (store accounts, payments, Account Summary, Store Statement). The parts were chosen together and would change together: how a store's account is stored, how its balance and the paid status of each invoice are produced, how a payment or an entry is kept exactly-once, how issuing and cancelling an invoice change the account, and how the Account Summary printed on an invoice is kept unchangeable. It follows the pattern of ADR-0003, ADR-0004, ADR-0005 and ADR-0006 and extends ADR-0006.

- **Status:** Accepted at the plan stage (the owner asked for this ADR on 2026-11-16). Implemented on 2026-11-16 as written, with migration `0008_store_accounts.sql` applied to the TEST project: the PGlite suites (56 files, 651 tests), the Supabase TEST tests for concurrency, isolation and scale, and the mobile browser specs pass; at 2,000 entries the account opens in about 0.5 s, the statement in about 1.9 s and a payment is saved in about 0.2 s. Also applied to the DEV project (the three existing invoices stay outside the account); not yet checked by the owner on a phone. Measured details are in `specs/007-store-accounts-payments/quickstart.md`, section 6. The decision is revisited if later measurements or modules disagree.
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Feature:** 007-store-accounts-payments
- **Related:** ADR-0006 (invoice issue and immutability: this ADR extends `invoice_issue` and `invoice_cancel` and relies on the guard that freezes an issued invoice), ADR-0005 (append-only entries, the entry id as the request id, one shared definition for counts and lists), ADR-0004 (replay-safe functions), ADR-0003 (function-only writes, select-only tables), ADR-0002 (REST handlers over RLS, client-generated ids).
- **Context:** Invoices are now issued and immutable, but the supplier has no record of money. Module 007 must give every store a running account (khata) and meet these rules together: the balance is never a stored or editable number; entries are never edited or deleted and a mistake is a reversal with a reason; only issued invoices create entries; a payment belongs to the store, is applied oldest unpaid first, keeps any excess as an advance, and can never reach another store; a retry, a double tap or two devices must never record a payment twice; issuing an invoice together with a payment received at the door is one atomic operation; the balance printed on an invoice (the Account Summary) is a snapshot that later payments never change; and paid, partly paid and unpaid are derived, never stored. The owner also decided that the khata starts empty (invoices issued before this module are not copied in), that small adjustments are in scope, and that an opening balance can be entered on any day. A stored-balance or "update the row" design, or a payments table that points at invoices, cannot meet these together.

## Decision

1. **One append-only table holds every kind of entry.** `ledger_entries` has the types opening_balance, invoice, payment, adjustment and reversal; a payment is an entry, not a row in a second table. Each entry has exactly one side (debit or credit), a date, the client key as its id, an identity number that records the order of recording, who and when, and a note or reason where the type needs one. Check constraints give each type its shape. A trigger refuses every update and delete (only the service role may delete, for test cleanup). Signed-in users have `select` only (Row Level Security); the only insert is one internal `SECURITY DEFINER` helper that no user can call.
2. **The store can never be wrong, by structure.** Composite foreign keys tie an entry to the same store as its invoice and as the entry it reverses (`unique (business_id, store_id, id)` on `invoices` and on the ledger). A payment cannot point at another store's invoice and a reversal cannot point at another store's entry, whatever code runs.
3. **The balance and the allocation are computed, never stored.** Balance = sum of debits minus sum of credits (positive = the store owes, negative = an advance). `ledger_allocation` settles the credit pool (payments, advance opening balances, reducing adjustments, none of them reversed) against the open debit items (opening balances, invoices, increasing adjustments, none of them reversed) with one window function, oldest first: opening balances first by their date, then everything else by `(date, order of recording)`. Reversed entries and their reversals are left out of both sides. It gives each invoice unpaid, partly paid or paid. An advance needs no special code: credit beyond all debts waits in the pool and settles the next debt that is added. The stores list, the Home total, the account screen, the payment preview, the statement and the invoice json all read the same two definitions (`ledger_balances`, `ledger_allocation`), so they cannot disagree.
4. **Exactly-once comes from keys the database owns.** Opening balance, payment, adjustment and reversal calls use the client-created UUID as the entry id: the same id with the same content returns the stored entry (`replayed`), with different content it is refused (`request_reused`). Partial unique indexes make a second invoice entry for one invoice, a second reversal of one entry, and a second payment received with one invoice impossible for any code path.
5. **Issue and cancel are extended inside their existing functions, with one lock order.** `invoice_issue` gains an optional payment: after its existing checks it validates the payment (before any number is taken), locks the store's ledger, reads the previous balance, takes the number, updates the invoice including the Account Summary figures, inserts the invoice entry and the optional payment entry, and writes the histories, all in one transaction. `invoice_cancel` adds one reversal entry for an invoice that is in the account and leaves any payment received with it untouched. A replay of either returns before any write. Every function that takes more than one lock takes them in the same order: invoice, order, the store's ledger, the invoice counter. Functions that touch only the ledger take only its lock. The previous balance therefore has an exact meaning: every entry recorded before this one, and nothing can slip in between.
6. **The Account Summary snapshot is part of the immutable invoice.** Four new columns on `invoices` (`in_account`, `summary_previous`, `summary_payment`, `summary_due`) are set by the same update that issues the invoice. The existing guard from ADR-0006 already refuses every change to an issued invoice except the cancellation columns, so these columns are frozen without changing any trigger (a test updates each column and expects a refusal). The snapshot is always saved at issue; the print screen only decides whether it is shown, so it can be turned on for a reprint without changing a number.
7. **The khata starts empty.** `in_account` defaults to false for every invoice issued before this module: those invoices show "Not in the account", have no paid status and no summary, and cancelling one adds nothing. Nothing is copied into the ledger. The opening balance form warns that those invoices must not be counted again.
8. **Dates keep old statements true.** Entries may be dated any day from 2000-01-01 to today (Pakistan time); the invoice entry takes the invoice date; a reversal and a cancellation entry take the day they are made, so a past statement is never rewritten. The statement and the running balance order entries by `(date, order of recording)`; a saved Account Summary reads the order of recording, so a later back-dated entry changes old running balances on the statement but never a summary.
9. **Reversal rules are enforced in the database.** A payment, an opening balance or an adjustment can be reversed once with a required reason; a reversal cannot be reversed; an invoice entry is reversed only by cancelling its invoice. A structural insert trigger repeats these checks as defence in depth.

## Consequences

### Positive

- No code path can rewrite money: every update and delete is refused by the database, and a correction is a visible reversal with a reason plus a new entry.
- A lost response, a double tap, a refresh or ten simultaneous requests give one entry, one invoice entry per invoice, one reversal and at most one payment per invoice; issue with a payment is all or nothing, including the invoice number.
- A payment can never reach another store, by the table's own keys, and business isolation is enforced in the functions and by Row Level Security.
- Nothing about balances or statuses is stored, so nothing can drift: a reversal or a later payment changes the next read correctly, and the Home total always equals the stores list.
- The Account Summary on an invoice can never change after issue, and no customer document can show cost, MRP or profit because no ledger or invoice table has such a column.
- Offline payments (Module 009) and reports (Module 010) can read the same entries and definitions without a schema change.

### Negative

- Rules in SQL again (migration `0008`), so every change is a migration with PGlite and Supabase tests, and a missing business filter in a `SECURITY DEFINER` function would be a leak. Mitigation: composite keys, a filter on every statement, two-user tests with crafted ids.
- The allocation runs on every invoice read and invoices list; it is exact but its cost grows with the number of entries. Mitigation: indexes, restricting the calculation to the stores on the page, and a scale test (2,000 entries, 100 stores, a 1,000-invoice list); a performance migration only if the goals are missed.
- Migration `0008` changes five Module 006 objects (the `invoices` table, `invoice_json`, `invoice_issue`, `invoice_cancel`, `invoice_list`) and replaces two function signatures, so the Module 006 suites must be rerun and existing row counts compared before and after.
- Every future function that takes more than one of these locks must keep the lock order, or a deadlock becomes possible. Mitigation: the order is written in the data model and a simultaneous issue-and-payment test covers it.
- Opening balances are settled before invoices even when their date is later, which matches the owner's rule but can surprise a supplier who meant a newer balance.
- Leaving old invoices out of the khata (the owner's choice) means a double count in an opening balance cannot be detected by the system; only the form warning protects against it.
- A statement can show running balances that differ from an older printed summary when a back-dated entry was added later; the statement says so.
- A payment received with a cancelled invoice stays on the account as an advance until the supplier reverses it separately; the cancel dialog says so.
- Online only: payments need a connection until Module 009; the app does not claim "saved on this device" or "pending sync".

## Alternatives Considered

- **A stored balance column per store (or a running-balance column on each entry).** Rejected: forbidden by the constitution, and a missed update shows a wrong balance.
- **A separate `payments` table (and an `adjustments` table) beside an invoice ledger.** Rejected: a second source of truth for money received, more joins and more places to make exactly-once and isolation true.
- **Allocate each payment to one invoice when it is recorded and store the allocation.** Rejected: the spec says a payment belongs to the store account; stored allocations would have to be rebuilt after every reversal and could drift.
- **Allocate by entry date alone (the opening balance by its "as on" date).** Rejected: an old balance typed as "as on today" would be settled after today's invoice, the opposite of the owner's rule and the master prompt ("the opening balance, then invoices by date").
- **Copy every old issued invoice into the ledger in the migration (recommended by the assistant).** Not chosen: the owner chose an empty khata and a self-entered opening balance. The cost is the undetectable double count described above.
- **A separate snapshot table for the Account Summary, or computing the summary again at print time.** Rejected: a second thing to freeze and an extra join on every print, or numbers that change when payments are recorded later.
- **A separate ledger function called by the application after `invoice_issue` and `invoice_cancel`.** Rejected: two transactions could leave an issued invoice with no entry (or the reverse) after a failure; one function is one transaction.
- **A shared replay table for payments.** Rejected: the entry's own primary key and the partial unique indexes already give exactly-once.
- **Dating a reversal like the entry it reverses.** Rejected: it would silently change a statement that was already printed.
- **Server-side PDF for the statement.** Rejected: a new dependency and out of scope; the browser's print and save as PDF are used.
